Sizing

Five bands, and one way to measure

Measure base to crown of the head — not including the mukut, and not including the singhasan or chowki underneath. That one rule is what makes the band right. The band decides the cloth and the stitching time, which is what the price follows.

Band S16″ and underSmall Laddu Gopal, travel murtis
Band S2Over 6″ up to 12″The most common home size
Band S3Over 12″ up to 18″Larger home shrines
Band S4Over 18″ up to 30″Big installations
Band S5Above 30″Measured and quoted individually

A seated deity and a standing one at the same height need different garments, so we ask which deity as well as which band; you do not have to work that out. Already know your size number — size 2, size 4 — from a poshak you have bought before? Tell us that instead and we will match it. Or send a photo with a scale beside the idol on WhatsApp and we will place it for you.

What a visit covers

  • Old cloth and nirmalya cleared and taken away.
  • The mandir cleaned — dusted and wiped down, and a clean asan laid.
  • Your deity dressed in the poshak you chose — or prepared and handed to you.
  • The shringar arranged as it should sit.
  • The old poshak collected and recycled respectfully, with a photograph of what it became.
  • A photograph of the finished mandir, even when you were not home.

We do not do fresh flowers or brass polishing. Flowers on a fortnightly visit look wrong within two days, and polish is chemical work on pieces that are often plated or inherited. We would rather do less, excellently, every time.

Our story

It started because someone else made it badly.

This family has been making dresses for deities since Saurabh’s father’s time. Deepika grew up around the work — the cutting, the sizing, the finishing, all of it happening in the house.

Then a batch was given out to a tailor and came back badly made. Mansi Agarwal refused it and made them herself. They were so much better that the orders came — more than one pair of hands could take, split between her and the tailors she trained.

It happened again years later. A dress ordered from the Muzaffarnagar market arrived with poor lining and cloth stiff enough to prick the deity. Same conclusion, second time: if it is going to be right, we make it ourselves. That is why we do not take open commissions — you choose from what we make, and what we make, we make properly.

We believe there is life in them. So nothing stiff, and nothing that pricks.

Why “Deepika”

Everyone assumes it is a person’s name. It is a name of the Devi — and it is the name our father gave his first business. We kept it so our parents’ thinking stays alive in this one.

Why now

Homes are smaller and days are busier. The mandir is still there, and most of us were never actually taught how to set one properly — not for lack of care, just for lack of anyone showing us. Families started asking us to keep the things at our place because they did not want to travel to the market, and it turned into everything under one roof.

Questions families ask

Straight answers

What is a dress combo pack?

Most homes have more than one deity. A combo pack lets you pick every one in your mandir — Laddu Gopal, Radha Krishna, Ram Darbar, Mata ji, Lakshmi Narayan, Ganesh ji, Sai Baba — give each one its size band, and receive custom dresses for all of them together, every month or every quarter, on one recurring plan.

Is the dress pack part of the visit plan?

No — they are two separate plans and you can take either on its own. The visit plan is priced by the size of your mandir and covers the clearing, cleaning, dressing and takeback. The dress combo pack is priced by the deities you choose and each idol’s size band. Households that take both get a bundled rate.

How do I get the size right?

Measure your deity from base to crown of the head — not including the mukut, and not including the singhasan or chowki underneath. Then pick the band: S1 is 6″ and under, S2 over 6″ up to 12″, S3 over 12″ up to 18″, S4 over 18″ up to 30″, and above 30″ is S5, quoted individually. A seated deity and a standing one at the same height need different garments, so we ask which deity as well. On the first visit we measure everything and keep it on file.

How do I choose the fabric each time?

Three weeks before each cycle we send you a link on WhatsApp. It shows what was sent last time, and this cycle’s fabrics and designs. You tap what you want, for each deity. About thirty seconds, and there is no phone call to answer.

Can I have a poshak made to my own design?

Every dress is custom in the way that matters — cut and stitched to your deity’s own measurements, not bought off a shelf. What you choose from is our set of fabrics and designs. An open commission means a one-off price and uneven quality, and we would rather show you five things we make beautifully than quote for something we have never stitched.

Why don’t you do flowers or brass polishing?

Because we would rather do less, excellently. Fresh flowers on a fortnightly visit look wrong within two days. Brass polishing is chemical work on pieces that are often plated, antique or inherited, and one damaged piece costs more than the subscription.

Who actually touches the idol?

You choose, and your choice is saved on your file. The default is that our sevadar prepares everything and hands the garment to a family member for the final dressing. Others ask us to do it fully. Both are entirely standard.

What happens to the old poshak you take away?

Returned garments are cleaned and reworked into asan and chandani for shrines, cut into wicks for temple lamps, used as covers for scripture, or ceremonially burnt with the ash returned to you. Deity cloth is never resold, shredded into rags, or made into anything worn on the body.

Which areas do you serve right now?

Visits are running in Noida Sector 107 only. We are starting in one sector deliberately, so every household gets the same standard. Dress combo packs are not limited by area — those are delivered anywhere in India. Leave your pin code and it decides where we open next.

Can I pause or cancel?

Pause any month from WhatsApp, and cancel any time before your next billing date. Festival packs can be rescheduled up to seven days before the reserved visit.

Can I become a sevadar?

Yes. We are onboarding sector by sector. You are trained on how a mandir is set, you serve households near your own home, and you are paid per visit. Sign up with your area and we will reach out.
