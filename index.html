The part everyone asks about first

Nobody touches your idol unless you ask them to. The default is that we prepare everything and hand the poshak to you for the final dressing — the seva stays yours. Say the word and we do it fully. It is your shrine and your call, recorded on your file so you never have to explain twice.

Built for apartments

Our Seva Sathis are background-verified, carry society-approved ID, wear shoe covers, work barefoot at the shrine, and are trained in what may and may not be touched. Visit windows are two hours so you are not waiting all morning, and you get a photograph of the finished mandir even when you were not home.

Questions families ask

Straight answers

Who actually touches the idol?

You choose, and your choice is saved on your file. The default is that our Seva Sathi prepares everything — cleans the mandir, presses the poshak, arranges the shringar — and hands the garment to a family member for the final dressing. Many families prefer this. Others ask us to do it fully. Both are entirely standard.

What happens to the old poshak you take away?

Returned garments are cleaned and reworked into asan and chandani for shrines, cut into wicks for temple lamps, used as covers for scripture, or ceremonially burnt with the ash returned to you. Every returned poshak gets a photo record showing what it became. Deity cloth is never resold, shredded into rags, or made into anything worn on the body.

How often should a deity's poshak be changed?

There is no single rule and traditions differ. In many Vaishnav households the poshak changes daily or on ekadashi and festival days. Most urban families realistically manage a monthly change plus something special for each major festival, and that is the rhythm our plans are built around.

How do I get the size right?

Measure your idol from base to crown in inches. Sizes 0–3 fit idols up to 6 inches, sizes 4–7 fit 6–12 inches, and anything larger is made to measure. Unsure? Send a photo with a scale beside the idol on WhatsApp and we will size it before dispatch.

Do you serve families outside India?

Yes. The seasonal poshak box ships worldwide with a prepaid return mailer for the old garment. And many customers abroad book Mandir Seva for their parents' home in India, pay in their own currency, and receive photographs after each visit.

Can I pause or cancel?

Pause any month from WhatsApp, and cancel any time before your next billing date. Festival packages can be rescheduled up to seven days before the reserved visit.

Which areas do you serve right now?

Gurugram, South Delhi, Noida and Dwarka. We open a new locality once enough families in it have joined the waitlist — adding your pin code genuinely moves it up the queue.
